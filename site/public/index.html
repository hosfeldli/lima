Lima is a free Mac browser where the Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode you already use works in real tabs beside yours, and nothing risky happens until you say yes.

Agents work in real tabs, and you can watch.

Each workspace is a folder in the sidebar with its own chat, tabs and folder on disk, and the sidebar shows what every agent is doing. Its agent reads, edits, runs and browses, and every step is listed where it happens. When an agent changes what it's working on, the tab you're reading stays put.

  • Your agents, unchanged. Lima drives the Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode CLI you already have signed in, with your model and plan.
  • Helpers in their own folders. A coordinating agent can split work across helper agents, each with its own chat, and they start when their dependencies finish.
  • Readable work. File edits show added and removed lines, commands show their result, and screenshots open full size.

Nothing risky happens without a yes.

Spending money, acting on an account, changing your Mac or signing in: when an agent reaches a step like that, it asks with a card that names who is asking and what for. The card stays until you answer, even if the agent's turn ends, and approving picks the work back up where it stopped.

  • Sign-ins too. When a site needs you to log in, the card links to the page and offers “I've signed in” for when you're done.
  • Outside AI tools are held to it. Tools connected over MCP can't read your chats, take screenshots or change settings until you allow it, one session at a time.

⌘T searches everything.

One box for the web, your open tabs, bookmarks, history, workspaces and notes. Ask Lima a question and the answer streams in place, or press ⌘↩ to hand the task to an agent.

See every agent at a glance.

The Companion takes a request, sends it to the right workspace and reports back. Its list follows your sidebar folders and shows only what needs a look: what's working, what's waiting on you and what's scheduled for later.

  • Schedule it. “Every Friday at 5, write my weekly report” becomes a scheduled task, confirmed with a card before it's created.
  • Remembers what you tell it. Personal memory is yours to read, edit or switch off.

The rest of your day lives in the sidebar too.

Lima is a full browser first, built on Zen and Firefox. The things you reach for between tasks sit in the same sidebar.

  • Notes and canvasesOne, two or three columns, tables and link previews, plus a canvas for sketching. Save a selection from any page with its source.
  • One music barSpotify, Apple Music and any tab playing audio, in one control at the bottom of the sidebar.
  • DictationPress ⌃⌥D to dictate, and the mic control sits in the dock.
  • Cleaner resultsHide AI answers and sponsored results on Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo and Brave, and YouTube Shorts. Each is a switch in Settings.

Your work stays on your Mac.

Lima has no account and no cloud copy. Notes, chats, workspace history and the Companion's memory are kept in an encrypted store whose key lives in your Keychain.

  • Your AI providerGets only the messages, files and page excerpts you send it. Lima doesn't proxy the request or keep a copy of what the provider receives.
  • Private windowsNever recorded, never shown to agents, never counted.
  • Page cleanupRuns in the browser and sends nothing.
  • Usage statisticsDaily feature counts only, on by default. Read each report before it's sent, or turn it off in one click.

Install in a minute.

  1. Download and open Lima.dmg, then drag Lima to Applications.
  2. Open Lima. It's signed with Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any Mac app. A short walkthrough brings over your browser data and connects your AI.
  3. That's it. Lima keeps itself up to date, and checks every update against a signed release feed before installing it.

Questions

Is Lima free?

Yes. Lima is free to download and use, and there's no account. AI features run on the Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode CLI you already use, under that provider's own plan.

Which AI agents does Lima work with?

Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode. Lima finds the CLIs installed on your Mac and drives them through its own tools, so they can work in browser tabs, ask you questions and request approval.

What does an agent need my approval for?

Anything Lima's policy flags: spending money, acting on an account, changing your Mac, new access, or a sign-in. The approval card names who is asking and what for, and it stays until you answer. When you approve, the agent picks up where it stopped.

Which Macs does Lima run on?

Macs with Apple Silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The download is about 230 MB.

Is Lima built on Chrome?

No. Lima is built on Zen, a Firefox-based browser, so it uses the Firefox (Gecko) engine and Firefox's security updates.

Does Lima send my data anywhere?

Notes, chats and workspace history stay on your Mac in an encrypted store. Your AI provider receives only what you send it. Anonymous usage statistics (daily feature counts, no content) are on by default and turn off in one click.
