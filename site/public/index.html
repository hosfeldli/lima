For the things you already do.

Not just code. If it happens in a browser, an agent can take the busywork while you keep going.

  • Build somethingAsk an agent to fix a website, run the tests and open the result, while you work in another tab.
  • Plan a tripResearch hotels and flights and keep the findings in a travel workspace, sorted into folders.
  • Find the right productCompare products across sites and collect reviews. Nothing is bought without your OK.
  • Get work doneResearch a topic, keep the sources together and get a draft report in a note.
  • Repeat it“Every Friday at 5, write my weekly report.” Scheduled work runs and waits for you when it's done.

Everything you expect from a browser.

Lima is a full browser first, built on Zen and Firefox. AI is there when you want it and out of the way when you don't.

  • Move in from your old browserBring bookmarks, history and saved passwords from Chrome, Safari, Edge, Brave and others. You choose what comes along.
  • Firefox add-onsYour favourite Firefox extensions install and run as they do in Firefox.
  • Tabs that stay organizedVertical tabs, folders and split view, with a sidebar that keeps work, reading and agents apart.
  • Mini LimaLinks you click in Mail, Messages or Slack open in a small window. Read and close it, or move it into Lima with ⌘O.
  • Firefox's protectionsThe same engine and security updates as Firefox, with tracking protection on. Lima updates itself automatically.
  • AI is optionalSkip AI setup and Lima is a fast, private browser with notes and search. Use it beside your current browser for as long as you like.

One assistant to coordinate. Workspaces to do the work.

Tell the Companion what you want. It hands the job to a workspace: a folder in your sidebar with its own agent, tabs and notes. You can watch, step in, or come back when it's done.

  1. AskType a request, or press ⌘↩ in search to hand it off.
  2. Lima organizes itThe request goes to the right workspace, and big jobs split into helpers.
  3. Watch it happenEach workspace tells its story: what it's doing now, each step, and what it made.
  4. Approve what mattersSensitive steps wait for you on a card that says who's asking and why.

The tools between tasks are built in.

No extra apps for notes, search or music. They live in the same sidebar, and your agents can use them too.

  • ⌘T searches everythingThe web, open tabs, bookmarks, history, files, workspaces and notes in one box. Ask a question for a quick answer, or ⌘↩ to hand it to an agent.
  • Notes that calculateSliders, switches and live results: a recipe that scales with guests, a budget that splits itself, checklist progress that updates as you tick.
  • One music barSpotify, Apple Music and any tab playing audio, in one control.
  • Dictation and grammarPress ⌃⌥D to dictate anywhere in Lima, and ⌘G to fix the grammar of selected text in any app once you allow it.
  • Cleaner search resultsHide AI answers and sponsored results on Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo and Brave, and YouTube Shorts. Each is a switch.

You're in control of sensitive actions.

When an agent reaches a step that matters, it stops and asks with a card that names who is asking and what for. The card stays until you answer.

  • What asks firstSpending money or placing a bet, entering a password, submitting a form, signing in, acting on one of your accounts, changing your Mac, or visiting a site outside the plan.
  • Permissions you can seeAccess you grant up front is listed in the workspace's chat, with a Revoke button.
  • Guardrails on every runA run stops after a set number of steps or minutes. Text an agent reads on the web is treated as information, not instructions, and any file it changes outside its workspace is listed.

Your data stays on your Mac, with clear exceptions.

Lima has no account and no cloud copy, and it collects no usage statistics or telemetry. Notes, chats and workspace history are kept in an encrypted store whose key is in your Keychain, with a week of encrypted backups.

  • Your AI providerReceives your messages and files, plus what agents read while working for you, such as pages they open. That's under your provider's terms; Lima doesn't proxy or copy it.
  • Companion memoryOnce a day, the Companion sends your recent requests and activity to your AI provider to write short notes about what you're working on. It's on by default; turn it off in Settings → Personal Memory.
  • Your search engineGets what you search for, like any browser. Google is the default for new installs; you can change it.
  • UpdatesLima downloads its signed updates without sending anything that identifies you.
  • Private windowsNever recorded and never shown to agents.

Get started in a few minutes.

A short walkthrough opens the first time you start Lima. Every step can be skipped.

  1. Install LimaDownload it, drag it to Applications and open it. It's signed and notarized by Apple.
  2. Bring over your browserImport bookmarks, history and passwords from the browser you use now.
  3. Connect your AI, or skip itLima finds Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode on your Mac, can install one for you, and checks that you're signed in.
  4. Choose your permissionsDecide on Companion memory and optional access like Files and Accessibility. Each can change later.
  5. Try a first taskA safe example: an agent reads a page and sums it up while you watch.

What AI costs

Lima is free. AI features use an agent you sign in to, with that provider's plan and pricing:

  • Claude Code with a Claude subscription or an Anthropic API key.
  • Codex with a ChatGPT plan or an OpenAI API key.
  • OpenCode with the model provider and key you choose.

You don't need a terminal: the walkthrough installs the agent and opens its sign-in.

About Lima

  • Who makes itLima is an independent, open-source project published by Liam Hosfeld under the MPL-2.0 license. The code is on GitHub.
  • Why it existsAI agents are most useful where your work already happens. Lima puts them in the browser, where you can see and steer them.
  • How it's paid forLima is free, with no paid tier. It shows no ads, sells no data and collects no telemetry.
  • Security updatesNew Zen and Firefox releases are rebuilt into Lima automatically. Every update is signed, checked before it installs, and rolls out gradually.

  • What's newEach update opens a What's New page in Lima. Release notes are on GitHub.

Questions

Do I need AI to use Lima?

No. Skip AI setup and Lima is a complete browser with notes and search. You can connect an agent any time in Settings.

Do I need to use the terminal?

No. If none of Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode is on your Mac, the walkthrough offers to install one and opens its sign-in.

Can I use my ChatGPT or Claude subscription?

Yes, through the agent that provider makes: Codex signs in with a ChatGPT plan, and Claude Code with a Claude subscription. Both also accept an API key. Lima doesn't charge for AI; your provider's plan does.

Can I bring my bookmarks and passwords?

Yes. The walkthrough, or File → Import From Another Browser, brings over bookmarks, history and saved passwords from Chrome, Safari, Edge, Brave and other browsers.

Do Firefox extensions work?

Yes. Lima is built on Firefox, so Firefox add-ons install and run normally.

Can I keep using my current browser?

Yes. Lima doesn't need to be your default browser. Many people try it beside Chrome or Safari first. If you make it your default, Mini Lima keeps links from other apps out of your way.

Can I use Lima without giving AI my browsing?

Yes. Agents only see the pages in their own workspace and what you share with them, never private windows. Companion memory, which sends a daily summary of your activity to your AI provider, can be turned off in Settings.

What happens if I quit Lima during a task?

Agents work while Lima is open. If you quit in the middle of a task, the agent stops; open Lima and tell it to continue. Scheduled reports can keep running with Lima closed if you turn on Run in background in Settings.

Does it use more battery?

Browsing works like the Firefox engine it's built on. Agents add work only while they're running, and the work itself happens in your AI provider's service.

Is Lima ready for daily use?

Lima is updated often and tested before every release, including an install over the previous version. Updates roll out over a day, and a release with a problem can be pulled. You can report anything from Settings → Help & support.

Which Macs does Lima run on?

Macs with Apple Silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The download is about 230 MB.
